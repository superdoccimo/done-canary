# Creator-first public coverage policy

DoneCanary was created by **美濃加茂まむ / @superdoccimo**.

## Don't erase the person who built it

Do not take someone else's open-source work, turn it into traffic, followers,
ad revenue, subscriptions, sponsorship value, or reputation, and then erase
the person who actually built it.

If your coverage of DoneCanary gets more attention than the original
developer, that makes the creator link more important, not less.

```text
Healthy:
creator builds → you introduce it → you get attention → readers can still find the creator

Unhealthy:
creator builds → you introduce it → you get the traffic → the creator disappears
```

DoneCanary does not consider the unhealthy path acceptable attribution.
Introducers may gain traffic, followers, revenue, subscribers, sponsorship
value, or reputation. The failure is taking that benefit while removing the
path back to the person whose work created the opportunity.

## The introducer is not the source

The person who introduces an open-source project is not the source of the
project.

- A blog post is not the canonical source.
- A viral X post is not the canonical source.
- A YouTube video is not the canonical source.
- A newsletter is not the canonical source.
- A mirror is not the canonical source.

The original repository and developer are the source. Third-party coverage may
attract more traffic than the creator. That does not transfer authorship,
provenance, or creator attribution to the introducer.

## Required credit for public coverage

If you publicly demonstrate, review, recommend, explain, redistribute official
media for, or otherwise cover DoneCanary, credit and link:

```text
DoneCanary by @superdoccimo
Canonical source: https://github.com/superdoccimo/done-canary
Developer: https://x.com/superdoccimo
```

Do not remove creator attribution from official project assets. Do not replace
the canonical source with your own landing page, redirect, mirror,
affiliate-style intermediary, or repost.

## Monetized coverage

If you make money, collect followers, gain subscribers, obtain sponsorship
value, or otherwise benefit from covering DoneCanary, do not erase the
developer who created the thing you are benefiting from. Commercial benefit
does not reduce the need for creator attribution. It increases the importance
of preserving the source.

## Recording distribution and attribution separately

Large reach and correct attribution are separate observations. A coverage
record may use this template:

```text
Third-party coverage: 500,000 views
Original developer traffic attributable or plausibly connected to that coverage: very small / none observable
Creator link present: NO

Classification:
DISTRIBUTION_SUCCESS
CREATOR_ATTRIBUTION_FAILURE
```

If the canonical repository is linked, `@superdoccimo` is credited, and the
developer X account is linked, the corresponding classification is:

```text
DISTRIBUTION_SUCCESS
CREATOR_ATTRIBUTION_SUCCESS
```

This record is an explicit public-coverage assessment, not telemetry collected
by the DoneCanary executable.

## 日本語 — 原作者を消さないでください

他人のOSSを紹介して、自分だけアクセスや収益を取る。その一方で、
実際に作った開発者へのリンクは消す。そんな都合のいい紹介を、
DoneCanaryではまともなcreator attributionとは考えません。

紹介者がアクセスを得ること自体は構いません。紹介記事や動画で収益を
得ることも構いません。しかし、その紹介が原作者より大きな注目を
集めるなら、なおさら原作者への導線を残すべきです。

紹介者だけが10万、100万とアクセスを集め、コードを書き、直し、
保守している原作者にはほとんど人が来ない。そんな構造はおかしい。
DoneCanaryを紹介するなら、必ずcanonical repositoryとoriginal
developerを明示してください。

```text
DoneCanary by @superdoccimo
GitHub: https://github.com/superdoccimo/done-canary
開発者X: https://x.com/superdoccimo
```

## Other open-source developers

This is not a special rule only for `@superdoccimo`. Many open-source
developers give away code, documentation, fixes, maintenance, compatibility
work, and support for free. Someone else may explain that work to a larger
audience and gain traffic, followers, revenue, or reputation while the actual
developer becomes invisible. That imbalance should not be treated as normal.

DoneCanary treats creator attention return as part of project provenance.
Other OSS maintainers are encouraged to reuse this policy.
